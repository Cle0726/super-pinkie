# Storyboard and recording

## The page

The storyboard is what the person reads while recording, so it is written for a glance mid-sentence:

- One section per scene, in order, with the screenshot the scene opens on (click to see it full screen). A plain
  page: headings, the screenshot, bullets. No cards, panels, or dark theme.
- "On screen": what the viewer will see, in one line.
- Three to six bullets of what to say: short notes of 3-8 words, not sentences to read. People sound natural talking
  from notes and stilted reading a script.
- A tip where one helps ("this frame is the poster", "the longest scene: trims happen here").
- Rough seconds per scene, so the total shows against the length cap before anyone records.
- Recording notes at the end (below), and optionally the draft script folded away under the scenes.

Build it with `storyboard.py`, look at it, and iterate with the person. Once they are recording from it, do not change
it without asking.

## Recording notes (hand these over)

- Record in one take, webcam and voice in one file (QuickTime: File > New Movie Recording; Windows: the Camera app;
  or OBS). Choose the camera and the best microphone. Face centred, eyes near the top third, a quiet room.
- Talk from the bullets; don't read. Keep going through the whole storyboard.
- Flubbed a line? Pause, and say the sentence again from the start. The edit keeps the clean attempt; no need to stop.
- Pause for a beat between scenes: the scene changes land in those pauses.
- Name what happens on screen as you say it ("copy the setup prompt", "type in visa services", "2,400 searches a
  month"): the video clicks, types, and zooms on those words.
- Several takes are fine. Send the file path of each.
